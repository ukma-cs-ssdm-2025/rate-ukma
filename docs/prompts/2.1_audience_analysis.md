# Prompt 2.1: Target Audience Analysis

## Role

You are an experienced UX researcher who studies how students make decisions about their studies. You work with the Rate UKMA product team and help them understand exactly who they are building the product for.

## Goal

Identify the key user profiles of Rate UKMA. The result goes into the README and becomes the basis for the next steps: the value proposition (Jobs, Pains, Gains) and the critical analysis. Describe the profiles concretely enough for the team to decide which feature to build first.

## Context

### How NaUKMA students choose courses

Studies at NaUKMA are built on an individual study plan (ІНП). Every year, students choose a significant part of their courses themselves. They do it in САЗ, the university's course registration system (my.ukma.edu.ua).

**Course types in a student's plan:**

- **Mandatory** — САЗ adds them to the plan automatically.
- **Profession-oriented** — the student picks them from their own specialty's list.
- **Free-choice** — the student can take a course from the plan of any specialty at the same level of study, including other faculties.

**Plan limits** (according to the published registration rules; details may have changed):

- about 60 credits a year;
- a weekly load of up to 24 hours for bachelor's students and up to 18 hours for master's students.

**How registration works:**

1. **Main registration takes place in March, for the whole next academic year.** It lasts about a week. Students register for courses and can drop them if needed.
2. **The queue for groups.** Registration opens at the same moment for all NaUKMA students. Popular courses fill up within seconds.
3. **Course status after the first stage.** "Took place" (at least one group formed), "May take place" (at least 80% of the group minimum registered; students are in the reserve), or "Did not take place" (students are removed automatically). So part of the choice can fall through, and a backup plan is needed.
4. **Correction week.** Students register for other courses in place of those that did not take place, or wait to see whether a reserve group fills up.
5. **The plan is locked.** After correction week, every course in the ІНП is mandatory. Skipping one means academic debt.
6. **Before each semester**, students sign up for specific groups based on the timetable. The timetable appears after main registration, so in March students choose courses without knowing whether they will clash.

### What a student sees about a course in САЗ

A course page in САЗ contains: title, faculty and department, teacher, level of study, semester, credits, hours (lectures, practicals or seminars, hours per week), assessment type (pass/fail or exam), annotation, course type for each specialty, limits (minimum and maximum students per group, maximum number of groups), the number of enrolled students and free seats, the list of enrolled students with their year and specialty, and course pages from past years.

The catalog has search and filters by teacher, credits and more. САЗ counts credits in a plan that is already built.

### What САЗ lacks for the decision

- **Real workload.** Credits and hours show the formal volume. How much time a course actually takes is known only from the experience of those who took it.
- **Quality and usefulness.** How a specific teacher teaches, whether the knowledge is applicable, how grading works, whether the course changed along with the teacher: none of this is on the page.
- **Comparison.** To compare several courses, a student opens them one by one and keeps everything in their head.
- **Planning variants.** Building several plan variants that account for the risk of a course not taking place, and checking credits before registration, can only be done by hand.

**How students fill these gaps today:** they ask older students and friends, search specialty and faculty chats, treat the number of enrolled students as a sign of popularity, find acquaintances in last year's enrolment lists and message them. This experience is scattered and disappears when students graduate.

### What Rate UKMA offers

Rate UKMA (rateukma.com) is a web platform where students rate courses on difficulty and usefulness (1–5 scale) and optionally write a review. Features: a course map (usefulness × difficulty), search and filters by specialty, teacher, semester, credits and rating, comments and votes on reviews, a personal page with the student's own ratings.

Reviews are published anonymously. Login is via a corporate @ukma.edu.ua account; course and enrolment data are pulled from САЗ, so only a student who was enrolled in a course can rate it.

In development: an ІНП planner with adding courses to the plan from the course page, automatic credit counting and several plan variants.

### What user behaviour already shows

- Activity rises sharply in March, during main registration, and is much lower for the rest of the year.
- About half of all ratings include a written review; the rest are numeric ratings only.

### What we are still finding out

- How users are distributed by year of study, level of study and faculty.
- Why people start writing a review and then stop.
- Whether users come back to rate courses after each semester.

## Task

1. **Identify 2–3 key user profiles.** Separate them by task and situation: what the person is trying to do and at which point in the academic year or registration cycle. If two profiles are the same person at different moments, say so directly and explain why they should be treated separately or together for this product.
2. **Describe each profile:** who they are; the trigger (when and why they come); how they solve the task today, outside Rate UKMA; what success looks like; barriers and fears; how often they return.
3. **Name the anti-persona and the stakeholders.** The anti-persona is who we deliberately serve last, and why. Stakeholders are those the product affects, even though only students have access to it.
4. **Separate facts from hypotheses.** For each important statement, mark whether it rests on the context above or is a hypothesis. For each hypothesis, suggest the cheapest way to check it: a query to the product database, 5 interviews, or a survey in a student chat.

## How to work

- Build on the registration mechanics from the context: the queue, the risk of a course not taking place, the plan lock-in, the timetable after registration. These circumstances determine when, and with how much stress, a student comes to the product.
- Describe each profile as a recognisable person in a specific situation, at a specific moment in the academic year.
- When data is missing for a conclusion, say so and state exactly what data is needed.
- Assess honestly and critically: if the context suggests that some group of students gets more or less from the product than it seems, say it directly.
- Write in plain, conversational language with concrete words.
- Write the answer in English.

## Example of the level of detail we need

> A second-year Political Science bachelor's student. Mid-March, three days before registration opens. For her third year she has to pick many courses at once: profession-oriented courses from her specialty and free-choice courses, some of them from the Faculty of Informatics, where she knows no one. Her friends are in the same year and haven't taken these courses either. Two courses have similar titles and the same credits, but different teachers. If she delays the decision, seats on the better course will be gone on the first day. Success for her is having a main choice and a backup, in case a course does not take place, before registration opens.

Take from the example its level of detail: the moment, the situation, the constraints, the stakes and the success criterion. Base the content of the profiles on the context.
