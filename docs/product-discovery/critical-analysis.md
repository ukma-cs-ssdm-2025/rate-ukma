# Rate UKMA: Critical Analysis

## Analysis

**Value and users.** About 1 250 registered users out of roughly 5 083 students means about a quarter of the university has an account (~25%). That's real reach for a student project, but it also means three out of four students have never signed up, and September's ~300 monthly active users are about 6% of all students. But the only usage numbers the team has are about *writing* (ratings), not *reading*. There is no data on how many students opened a course page before registration, or whether anything they read changed their choice. The core value of the product, helping people decide, is currently unmeasured.

**Seasonality.** Ratings by month add up to about 1 750. March (794) and April (~365) make up about two thirds of that. February has ~5 and September ~15. December (~250) is a clear second peak at the end of the autumn semester. The March peak is explained: students rate past courses while planning their own year. The December peak's cause isn't stated in the context (end-of-semester habit, a campaign, or something else), and it matters, because it's the only proof that contributions can happen outside March.

**Two-sided supply.** 1 700 ratings spread across 520 courses is about 3.3 ratings per rated course. That's an average, so many pages probably have one or two. With groups of 10–25 students, even a "well-rated" course is based on a handful of opinions. The total number of courses at NaUKMA isn't given, so coverage (what share of courses have any rating at all) is unknown. 1 700 ratings from 1 250 users is about 1.4 ratings per user, while each student takes many courses a year, so most users rate little or nothing.

**Data and САЗ.** Everything that makes Rate UKMA trustworthy (verified enrolment, course data, the enrolled count) comes from a scraper. Loading is manual and takes a developer 2–3 hours, and data isn't pulled often, which already rules out features like free seats or cancellation updates during correction week. There's no mention of an agreement with the university for this access.

**Legal, privacy and ethics.** To enforce "only enrolled students can rate" and "one rating per course," the product has to store enrolment data and link each review to a real account. So anonymity exists only on the public side. Enrolment lists likely include students who never signed up. There's no mention of a privacy policy, data retention rules or a reporting and moderation process for reviews about named teachers.

**Institutional.** The administration and the САЗ team control both the login and the data. The context shows no relationship with them, positive or negative. That's the thinnest piece of evidence in the whole picture, attached to the highest-impact dependency.

**Roadmap.** The three initiatives are reasonable, but their order matters. The planner only creates value if it's live before March registration. Analytics are needed to judge the planner, and the pipeline is needed for fresh data in March. The evidence doesn't yet show that students want to plan outside САЗ, which already counts credits for a built plan.

**Team and sustainability.** Five students from one faculty, working within university courses, with AI writing part of the code and documentation. There's no stated plan for what happens after graduation or after the course that currently hosts the project ends.

**Measurement.** The team cannot see drop-off, page views or the effect of any change. With one real decision window a year, a missed measurement in March 2027 means waiting until March 2028 to learn anything.

### Weak assumptions

| Assumption | Where it comes from | What would confirm or refute it |
|---|---|---|
| Students who read reviews change their course choices because of them. | Value proposition, all three profiles | Post-registration survey: "Did Rate UKMA change what you registered for?" A meaningful share of "yes" confirms it; mostly "no, I'd chosen already" refutes it. |
| A few reviews are enough to help a decision ("works when the course has at least a few reviews"). | Value map, Profiles 1 and 2 | 5 interviews: show a course page with 2 reviews and one with 8, and ask how confident the student would be. If 2 reviews don't change confidence, the threshold is higher. |
| Profile 1 students (second-year bachelors planning their third year, cross-faculty choosers) are a significant share of users. | Audience analysis | DB query: users by year, level and faculty, and views of courses outside the user's faculty (needs analytics). |
| Students want to plan variants in Rate UKMA and then re-enter them in САЗ. | Roadmap, planner | Before building beyond the minimum: 5 interviews or a chat survey on how students plan today. After launch: how many users create a second variant. |
| Fear of being identified is a real barrier to writing reviews. | Audience analysis, Profile 3 | Chat survey: "Did you ever decide not to write a review? Why?" with anonymity as one option. |
| Reminders at the end of a semester will increase supply. | Backlog, value map | The December peak (~250) is the natural test: find out what caused it. If it was a campaign, repeat it in January and compare. |
| Contributors are motivated by helping others and by seeing their review was useful. | Audience analysis, value map | DB query: do users who got votes or comments on a review rate more courses afterwards than those who didn't? |
| The course count, not just the user count, gives meaningful coverage. | Implicit in "~520 courses rated" | DB query: total courses in САЗ for the current year, and the share with 0 and with 5+ ratings. |
| The university tolerates the product. | Implicit: nothing in the context mentions the administration's view | The first conversation with the administration or the САЗ team. |
| Registered users are a meaningful share of students. | Traction data (~1 250 users) | Refuted in part: with ~5 083 students, that's about 25%, not the third implied by earlier secondary sources. A DB query by faculty and year shows which groups the other 75% come from. |
